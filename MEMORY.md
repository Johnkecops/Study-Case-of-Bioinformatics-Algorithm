# MEMORY: IKSAD Bioinformatics Algorithms Proceeding

## 4 September 2026, Manuscript type and scope
**What was decided:** Expand the i3L algorithm-review deck and its abstract into a REVIEW paper for the IKSAD
proceeding template, covering three application areas (CRISPR-Cas editing, machine-learning drug discovery,
bacteriophage design) compared at the level of the objective function.
**Why:** Comparing the three areas by their governing equations exposes a shared computational skeleton
(score plus search plus constraint) that separate reviews of each area do not surface.
**What was rejected:** Treating it as a primary research paper; treating the three areas in isolation.

## 4 September 2026, Review method
**What was decided:** Rapid review reported under PRISMA 2020, single database (PubMed/MEDLINE), three Boolean
blocks, window 2012-01-01 to 2026-09-04, screening pool capped at the 60 highest-ranking records per block.
Real counts: 18,991 records, 18,868 unique, 178 screened, 84 full-text, 21 included from searching plus 44 from
citation chaining, 65 total.
**Why:** A full systematic screen of 18,868 records is outside the scope of a conference proceeding. The cap is
declared in Methods and revisited in Limitations rather than hidden.
**What was rejected:** Reporting invented PRISMA numbers; claiming multi-database coverage that was not run.

## 4 September 2026, Reference verification procedure
**What was decided:** Every reference verified twice before inclusion: PubMed ESummary by PMID for authoritative
metadata, then Crossref DOI resolution with a title-similarity comparison. Nine candidate records were dropped
because the retrieved record was not the intended source. Log written to `reference_validation.md`.
**Why:** Metadata is taken from the services rather than from recall, which removes the hallucinated-citation
failure mode entirely. All 65 references resolve in both services.
**What was rejected:** Citing from memory and spot-checking a sample.

## 4 September 2026, Length target changed mid-task
**What was decided:** Target moved from 10 pages to 20 pages at the user's request. Final document is exactly
20 pages as rendered by Microsoft Word.
**Why:** The 10-page budget forced cutting roughly half the body text; 20 pages carries the full argument.
**What was rejected:** Padding to length. The extra space was filled with substantive paragraphs on screen
analysis, phage-specific practical constraints, and reproducibility reporting.

## 4 September 2026, Build is reproducible from source
**What was decided:** The DOCX is generated by scripts in `build/`, not edited by hand. `build/content.py` holds
the prose with `{PMID}` citation placeholders; `build/refs.py` renders APA 6 from verified metadata;
`build/omml.py` emits Word-native equations; `build/audit.py` enforces the citation rules.
**Why:** Any change to the text or the reference set re-runs the audits automatically, so the citation
invariants cannot silently break.
**What was rejected:** Hand-editing the DOCX, which would decouple it from the verification pipeline.

## 25 September 2026, Streamlit companion dashboard built from the proceeding
**What was decided:** Build a four-tab Streamlit dashboard (`app.py`, `algos.py`, `ncbi.py`,
`tests/test_algorithms.py`) that runs Equations 1-9 and Boxes 1-3 on small curated sets of real NCBI
records: five H37Rv drug-resistance genes via E-utilities, twelve real antibacterial agents via PubChem
PUG-REST, and five real genomes for the phage panel. The previous `plan.md` was renamed
`plan-manuscript.md`; the new `plan.md` covers the dashboard.
**Why:** The paper's claim that three areas share one computational skeleton is more convincing when a
reader can move the constraint weight and watch the ranking change. Real records only, because a
simulated demonstration would not show the real PAM density, real off-target load or real chemistry.
**What was rejected:** Biopython as a dependency (plain HTTP to E-utilities is enough); rdkit and the
QED score (not installed, a distance penalty over real PubChem descriptors covers Equation 6);
Streamlit multipage `pages/` (four tabs cover it); generated or simulated sequence data.

## 25 September 2026, Three modelling gaps declared on screen rather than filled with plausible numbers
**What was decided:** Where a component of an equation cannot be computed honestly offline, the panel
says so on screen and the value is not invented. Equation 2 ships illustrative monotonic positional
weights, not the Doench 2016 per-position-per-substitution table. Equation 4 ships no affinity model at
all, so the column reads "not modelled" and the lambda slider drives ranking through the constraint
term alone. Equation 9's host fitness term f is a declared GC-match proxy. Each is marked in source
with a `ponytail:` comment naming the ceiling and upgrade path.
**Why:** The paper's own argument is that reporting practice, not model capacity, limits
reproducibility. A dashboard that fabricated a CFD table or an affinity net to look complete would
contradict the paper it accompanies.
**What was rejected:** Reproducing the CFD weight table from recall; shipping an untrained affinity
model; omitting the affected panels entirely (the constraint term is demonstrable without them).

## 25 September 2026, Phage likelihood negative result kept and displayed
**What was decided:** The order-k Markov model scores the non-phage control (*Y. pestis* plasmid
pPCP1, 45.3% GC) ABOVE the held-out phage T4 (35.3% GC) at k = 1, 3 and 5, because the training
genomes run 48.4-63.5% GC and the model learns composition rather than phage identity. Kept as-is,
displayed in the panel, tabulated in the README, and asserted by
`test_eq8_is_confounded_by_composition` so that substituting a real genome LM makes the check fail
loudly.
**Why:** It demonstrates the paper's point about unanchored plausibility scores better than a tuned
success would. Measured values: T4 -1.4329 vs control -1.3982 nats/base at k = 5.
**What was rejected:** Changing the training set, the model order or the control until the separation
looked right; deleting the check; reporting the result only in a comment.

## 25 September 2026, Citation identifiers printed as supplied, with a resolution note
**What was decided:** The three motivating references appear in the README exactly as the author
supplied them, followed by a note recording what was checked on 25 September 2026: reference 1 resolves
in Crossref (pages 177-184); reference 2's DOI `10.11594/jbbi.12.1` does NOT resolve (404, issue-level
address under the journal's former registrant prefix) while the article is registered at
`10.55981/jbbi.2025.9769` with matching title, volume, issue and pages; reference 3 resolves through
Zenodo and DataCite rather than Crossref, as expected for a deposit, with matching metadata.
**Why:** The author chose to print the identifiers as given rather than have them silently corrected,
so the correction belongs in a visible note.
**What was rejected:** Substituting the resolving DOI into the citation; printing the citations with no
note on resolution.

## 25 September 2026, Sandbox blocks local port binding
**What was decided:** The dashboard was verified by executing `app.py` in Streamlit bare mode plus the
eleven offline checks, not by opening it in a browser. `streamlit run` cannot start inside the Claude
sandbox: binding a local port fails with `[Errno 1] Operation not permitted`.
**Why:** Recorded so a future session does not re-diagnose this as an application fault. Adding
`sandbox.network.allowLocalBinding: true` to settings would permit it without a restart.
**What was rejected:** Disabling the sandbox to launch a long-running server.
